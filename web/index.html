Small overseas buyers and India's farmer collectives can trade directly. The hard part is trust and payment.

Sauda gives each side an AI agent. The buyer's agent works under a signed spending mandate; each farmer producer organisation (FPO) has a sales agent that knows its private floor price and talks to the FPO manager in Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or Malayalam.

The agents negotiate. Code enforces the limits. A human approves. PayPal moves the money: the buyer's advance through Checkout, each FPO's share through Payouts the moment it proves dispatch, short shipments through Refunds, and the balance through a PayPal Invoice.

1Mandate. The buyer describes the purchase in plain words; the agent drafts a structured mandate; the buyer edits and signs it (HMAC).
2Negotiate. Buyer agent and FPO agents bargain round by round. Prices outside the mandate or below a seller's floor are clamped by code.
3Approve. The buyer sees the basket, the policy checks and the exact PayPal request, then approves on Sauda and again on PayPal.
4Pay on proof. An FPO reports dispatch in its own language; tracking is attached to the PayPal capture and its share is paid out.
5Settle. Shortfalls are refunded, the balance is invoiced, and the buyer confirms receipt to release the rest in one Payouts batch.

Architecture

Safety model

  • Models propose, code decides. The model never sees PayPal credentials and cannot call PayPal. It returns JSON that is validated, clamped and policy-checked.
  • Signed mandate. Budget, per-kg caps, seller count, certifications, advance and auto-release limit are HMAC-signed by the buyer; any change invalidates the signature and blocks payments.
  • Human in the loop. Every collection needs the buyer's approval here and on PayPal. Releases above the mandate's limit, and the final balance release, need the buyer. Small verified releases follow the mandate's own rule.
  • Integrity. Order, refund, payout and invoice amounts are recomputed from agreed prices and reported quantities, and re-checked right before execution. Payees can only be the FPO's registered PayPal account.
  • Idempotency. Every PayPal write carries a deterministic PayPal-Request-Id; Payouts use it as sender_batch_id; failed actions retry with the same key.
  • Sandbox lock and kill switch. No live PayPal URL exists in the code; PAYPAL_ENV other than sandbox stops the app. SAUDA_PAYMENTS_FROZEN blocks all money movement.
  • Prompt-injection resistance. Messages from the other party and from FPO managers are treated as data; they can change words, not amounts or payees.

Seller names, members, prices and stock are fictional sample data. PayPal runs in sandbox mode only; fixture mode simulates PayPal locally when no sandbox keys are configured and is labelled everywhere it appears.
